Use case

A work order is service on a unit.

A work order is its own record. It holds the service: the work done to a particular machine. It points at the asset. Through that asset you reach the cabinet, the casino the unit is at, and the parts that cabinet takes.

It is not a project. A project changes the floor — an install, a removal, a move, a conversion. A work order is service on a unit that is already a record. They can refer to the same asset. They do not become the same record.

On the casino, a work order is service at that property, because the asset is on that floor. On the asset, it is the service history of that machine. On the agreement, you still reach service through the asset the agreement covers, rather than pasting a second history onto the deal.

The parts stay on the cabinet. The work order points at the asset, and the asset points at the cabinet, so the parts for that service are the parts that cabinet already has.

Your database stays on your servers. Your data.
