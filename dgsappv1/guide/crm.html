Talk about the floor the customer actually has.

A customer conversation breaks when the agreement describes one set of machines and the building has another. The serials were written onto the deal once, and every install and pull since then lived somewhere else. The meeting becomes a reconciliation.

The property, the agreement, and the vendor point at the same units that performance and maintenance use. You can talk about what is installed, how it has done, which jobs moved machines on or off that agreement, and what service those units have had. A company that builds games, places them, and sells parts is having one conversation, not three systems.

The benefit is a commercial relationship that stays true as the floor changes. The deal does not go stale the day the next bank is installed. The customer sees that you know their floor, because it is the same floor your own performance and service are using.
