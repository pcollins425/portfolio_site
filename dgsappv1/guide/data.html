Your floor should stay yours.

People see collinsmediallc.com and assume the machines, the deals, and the results were handed to the company that hosts the page. That worry is fair. Which casinos you serve, which themes earn, and how your floors are serviced is competitive information. Holding it locally means that information does not become someone else’s asset.

The database stays on your servers. This site is the door, so approved people can open the application. It is not a copy of your database. Your people sign in with your company’s email, and that sign-in opens your server only. Another company using this same door is sent to its own server. Its login can open only that server. They do not receive your floors, your agreements, or your service history.

Ownership is also what makes the connection safe. Performance, maintenance, customers, and jobs are useful because they share one database. That is only a benefit if the database is yours. You can keep the job next to the result, and the service next to the machine, without placing that whole picture on a website you do not control.

If you stop using the door, the system of record is still on your servers. The shared site is the directory that points a company at that server.
