How the data fits together.

This is the technical picture of the application. A serial is an asset. A placement puts that asset on a floor for a span of time. A month is the result of the placements that were there. A job is why the floor changed. Service is work on a unit that was already there. The customer and the agreement describe that same floor.

Each section ends with the read another system can call. The call is on that company’s server, and it uses that company’s sign-in. A person can make it today. A system such as QuickBooks, calling for commission with its own key, would use the same reads. That key is not issued yet. Some reads also require that the signed-in person is allowed to see that area.

A signed-in account also shows how that account’s own records are set up, under each section.

The machine

A serial, a cabinet, and a theme are one asset. Performance, a job, a repair, and an agreement all point back to that asset. When two screens disagree about a machine, the first check is whether they are looking at the same serial.

The feed is a record of the physical unit. It lands once. Later screens read it. They do not keep a second copy of the serial.

Read

GET /api/assets

The assets: serial, cabinet, and theme.

GET /api/assets/hub/{asset_id}

One asset, and the records that hang off it.

The floor

A placement is that asset at a property, for a span of time. The floor at the end of a month is the set of placements still open. A conversion or a swap ends the old placement and starts the next one. The serial may stay. The placement does not. Counting both invents a machine.

The feed is the placement: which asset, which property, when it arrived, and when it left. The screen groups those placements by property.

Read

GET /api/slot-master/casinos

Properties, and how many placements are active at each.

GET /api/slot-master/machines?casino_id=

The placements at one property: the asset, and the dates that placement covers.

The month

Performance is a period of results for the placements that were there. Coin-in, win, and commission belong to a placement for that month. A figure that cannot be tied to a placement is unfinished, because nobody can say which machines produced it.

The month arrives as a report for a property. It lands against the placements. The screens read the sums, and they read how many of the expected lines have a report.

Read

GET /api/executive?month=YYYY-MM

Coin-in, actual win, and commission for the month, the change from the month before, and a trailing year of machines, jobs, and reporting.

GET /api/finance/overview?from=YYYY-MM&to=YYYY-MM

Expected participation lines against invoiced lines. This is the commission read another system would call.

GET /api/performance/themes-top?month=YYYY-MM

Themes for that month, on the placements that carried them.

The job

An install, a removal, a move, or a conversion is why the floor changed. The job names the property, the assets, and the dates. The next month can be read beside that job, so a movement in the numbers has a cause sitting on the same machines.

The feed is the job itself. It lands on the assets it touched. A repair of a machine that was already there is a different record, covered under service.

Read

GET /api/projects/catalog

The jobs, the property, and how many machines each job moved.

GET /api/projects/ims?casino_id=

The schedule of jobs at one property.

GET /api/projects/catalog/{reference_key}

One job, including the machines on it.

Service

Service is work on a unit that is already on the floor. The parts belong to that cabinet, so the list of parts is the list that cabinet takes. A warehouse holds a cabinet that is off the floor. The same asset identity is used, so a repair and a performance figure are about one machine.

The work order itself is kept by the maintenance connection. This application reads the asset, the parts, and the warehouse. It does not keep a second work-order book.

Read

GET /api/parts-inventory

Parts, and the cabinets they belong to.

GET /api/warehouse-inventory/pivot

Cabinets by warehouse, for units that are off the floor.

The customer

The property, the agreement, and the deal describe the same floor. The agreement covers placements. The deal is the commercial conversation about those machines. When a job changes the floor, the agreement and the deal are supposed to follow, because they point at the same assets.

The property feed is the house itself. Contacts and deals arrive from the CRM connection and land on that property. The agreement is the contract on the machines.

Read

GET /api/commerce/casinos/{reference_key}

One property: the house, its contacts, and its deals.

GET /api/commerce/deals

Deals across properties.

GET /api/contracts

Agreements, and the machines they cover.

Connections

These are the systems the product can attach to. A company turns on the ones it uses. The reads above stay on that company’s server either way.

System What moves
eMaint Floor and service. The machines and the work on them can be read, and a floor change can be written back.
HubSpot Customers, contacts, and deals. They are read into the commercial picture, and catalog quantity can be pushed the other way.
Google Sign-in with the company’s email, which opens only that company’s server. Gmail brings reports and paperwork in, and sends notices out.
