Electronic gaming operations

Electronic gaming machines, table games, and parts

Every record is its own entity.

For companies that build electronic gaming machines and electronic table games, place them, or sell the parts that keep them running — including companies that do more than one of those. A vendor is a record. A cabinet and its specs are a record. A particular machine on a floor is a different record, even when it is that same cabinet. A casino, an agreement, a theme, and a parts list are records too.

For people who already have access.

The facts stay with the thing they describe.

A cabinet’s specs stay the cabinet’s specs when a unit moves. A vendor stays that vendor when a new theme ships. What changes is the connection: this asset is at this casino, under this agreement, running this theme, built as this cabinet, from this vendor.

That connection is a reference. One record points at another real record. This is a relational database: separate entities, linked where the work needs the link, so a question can walk from one to the next.

You follow the question.

Getting to the information follows the way someone already thinks. Each stop is the next question, and it opens the record that question is about.

  1. You look up a casino. From there you can see how it has performed, open the floor, and look at what has been there.
  2. You pick a machine on that floor. From there you can see how that cabinet has performed, which theme is on it, and open the record for that specific asset.
  3. From the asset you can see the cabinet it is, the agreement it sits on, and the parts that cabinet takes.
  4. From the theme you can see where else it is installed.

Your data stays on your server.

This site is collinsmediallc.com. That address is the door.

Your machines, agreements, warehouses, and parts sit in a database on your own servers. People sign in with your company's email, and that sign-in opens your server only. Another company using this same door is sent to its own server. Its login can open only that server.
